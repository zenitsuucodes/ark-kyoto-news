第二次世界大戦後、強い絆で結ばれた信頼できる同盟国である日米：高市氏

TOKYO - Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Friday that Japan and the United States have become "allies bound by very strong ties" based on mutual trust after World War II, following a U.S.-China summit that noted the two nations' wartime cooperation.

Takaichi, meanwhile, told reporters that she has "no comment" on the meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Washington on Thursday.

----------

Japan PM says Trump voiced concern over weaker yen impact on U.S. trade

TOKYO - Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Friday that U.S. President Donald Trump expressed concern at their recent summit that a weaker yen is hurting U.S. trade.

Takaichi also said she told Trump during their meeting Tuesday in New York that an undervalued yen is generally a problem. She made the remarks to reporters at her office.

----------

Japan law banning under-18s from election campaigning unconstitutional

TOKYO - A Japanese court on Friday ruled that the law banning those aged under 18 from taking part in election campaigning is unconstitutional.

The Tokyo District Court said the law constitutes a violation of their right to freedom of expression, but dismissed a claim for damages from the state by four plaintiffs, including high school students.

----------

Japan to hold 1st expert panel talks on anti-espionage measures on Mon.

TOKYO - The Japanese government said it will hold the first meeting of an expert panel on Monday to discuss measures to boost intelligence capabilities including counterespionage legislation.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, known for her hawkish views on security, will attend the planned meeting, at which the creation of a foreign intelligence agency is also likely to be among the agenda items, the government said Friday.

----------

Nissan to maintain stake in Mitsubishi Motors after end of lockup period

TOKYO - Nissan Motor Co. is expected to maintain its current stake in Mitsubishi Motors Corp., even after the lockup period ends in October, sources familiar with the matter said Friday.

Nissan took a 34 percent stake in Mitsubishi Motors in October 2016 and then reduced it to 26.67 percent as of the end of March. The 10-year lockup deal restricts Nissan from selling its stake.

----------

Aug. supermarket, duty-free dept. store sales remain on upward trend

TOKYO - Duty-free sales at Japan's department stores in August rose 4.6 percent from a year earlier, marking the sixth consecutive month of growth, while nationwide supermarket sales inched up 0.2 percent for the second straight month of increase, indicating consumer spending remains intact, industry data showed Friday.

According to the Japan Department Stores Association, overall duty-free sales rose to around 46.1 billion yen ($292 million), due to a steady demand for luxury goods amid a weak yen, while average spending per customer jumped 15.6 percent to about 103,000 yen.

----------

Taiwan slams Xi's remarks urging U.S. to oppose island's independence

TAIPEI - Taiwan's Foreign Ministry on Friday rebuked Chinese President Xi Jinping over his remarks urging the United States to oppose the island's independence, calling them a "distortion of the facts" and saying only the people of Taiwan can decide the territory's future.

The ministry said sovereignty over Taiwan belongs to its people and that Beijing has no right to represent them, adding that the island's political future can only be "determined collectively by the people of Taiwan through democratic means."

----------

Xi urges U.S. to oppose Taiwan independence in talks with Trump

WASHINGTON - Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday urged the United States to oppose "Taiwan independence" and handle the issue of the self-ruled island with caution in his talks with U.S. President Donald Trump at the White House, according to the Chinese Foreign Ministry.

His call indicated Beijing's stronger pressure over Taiwan on Washington, which maintains it does "not support" the island's independence. China claims Taiwan as part of its territory and aims to achieve its unification with the mainland, by force if necessary.
