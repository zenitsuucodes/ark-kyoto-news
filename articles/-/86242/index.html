トランプ氏と習氏は友情を称賛する一方、厄介な問題ではほとんど進展なし

By Takuya Karube,ARK KYOTO NEWS
- Sep 25, 2026 - 12:27 全て,世界

WASHINGTON - U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday touted their friendship, as they spent hours together at the White House, but apparently made limited progress on thorny issues that fuel rivalry between their countries, such as trade and artificial intelligence.

Trump told reporters he had a "great meeting" with Xi. Yet neither Trump nor his administration released any concrete agreements as of near midnight, after a raft of elaborate ceremonies capped with a state dinner joined by Jensen Huang of AI chip titan Nvidia Corp. and other American technology leaders.

One closely watched point was whether and to what extent they could agree on managing AI risks. China's Foreign Ministry said Xi told Trump that they could cooperate more in the field.

"The right thing to do is to draw on each other's strengths, not guard against each other," the ministry quoted Xi as saying.

Xi, who traveled to the United States for his first state visit in 11 years, said AI must remain under human control, and China is willing to facilitate dialogue with the United States on the technology's risks and benefits, according to the ministry.

The primary goal of the summit was to maintain a stable U.S.-China relationship, officials of both countries had said, even as many disagreements remain, including on critical minerals, Taiwan and Iran.

At Xi's arrival ceremony, Trump said he had forged a "truly great friendship" with the Chinese president, based on "mutual respect and the vital interests of our people."

Xi, meanwhile, said the two countries should coexist in peace by expanding cooperation and promoting communication.

"China and the United States, as two major countries, stand to gain from cooperation and will both lose in confrontation," he said. "We do not need to avoid mentioning competition, but our competition should be a healthy one and should be kept within bounds."

Ahead of Xi's appearance at the White House, Trump wrote on social media that he wanted to leave AI "exactly where it is," indicating he remained against taking steps to regulate the technology's development. "That is China's position also," he added.

As China and the United States continue to compete aggressively for dominance in frontier AI models amid rising safety fears, Xi said at the ceremony that both countries have "the capability and responsibility" to develop and manage the technology for good as they drive its advancement.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told reporters on Sunday that Washington had proposed to Beijing the creation of a notification system for AI incidents that could impact national security.

Xi's state visit, coming about four months after Trump's trip to Beijing, underscores the mutual desire to continue high-level engagement despite the intensifying political, economic and technological rivalry between the two countries.

From the start of Xi's three-day visit, Trump has shown warmth toward the Chinese leader, greeting him personally with an extraordinary airport reception.

On the eve of the summit, Bessent said the United States and China would extend their fragile trade truce by two months to Jan. 10. It was due to expire on Nov. 10.

Trump and Xi agreed to pause their trade war when they met last October in South Korea. Their deal included a delay in China's new export controls for rare earth minerals needed to manufacture high-tech products.

The extension of the economic ceasefire was seen as essential to the ongoing broader detente.

With November's midterm elections on the horizon, Trump appeared eager to show domestic audiences his diplomatic acumen in dealing with China, the largest country outside the United States economically and, in Washington's view, its most consequential national security challenge.

On the economic front, U.S. officials had hoped that China would promise to buy more farm products and hinted at moving forward with reciprocal tariff reductions on some goods in nonsensitive sectors.

So far, no specific economic deals have been announced, apart from the truce extension.

Many experts on U.S.-China ties did not expect Trump to strike any sort of grand deal with Xi that could help shore up his approval ratings, battered by the economic fallout of his war against Iran as disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz have driven up gasoline prices at home and abroad.

Trump, who last held lengthy in-person talks with Xi in mid-May, continues to seek Chinese help in pressuring Iran as the war drags on with no apparent end in sight. But China, Iran's most important economic partner, has shown little interest in taking any action that could be seen as siding with the United States.

In addition to the nearly seven-month-old conflict that has raised inflation risks, Trump and Xi discussed Russia's war against Ukraine and North Korea, according to the Chinese ministry.

As always, Xi brought up Taiwan, a democratic island which China claims as part of its territory.

Xi said it is hoped that the United States will "adhere to the correct position of opposing Taiwan independence" and handle the Taiwan issue prudently.

China has not ruled out using force to bring the self-ruled island under Beijing's control.

U.S. allies and partners in Asia have grown concerned that Trump could reduce Washington's long-standing support for Taiwan's defense in exchange for economic agreements with China.

Such anxieties about the reliability of U.S. commitments to regional security were heightened after Trump described, in an interview at the time of his trip to Beijing, a pending weapons sale to Taiwan worth about $14 billion as a "negotiating chip" with China.
