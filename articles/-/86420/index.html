アジア大会：男子マラソンは山下が優勝、女子はハブテが優勝

By Morichika Nakamoto,ARK KYOTO NEWS
- Sep 26, 2026 - 13:07 全て,アジア競技大会,スポーツ

NAGOYA - Japan's Ichitaka Yamashita won the men's marathon at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games on Saturday, clocking 2 hours, 10 minutes, 49 seconds.

The 29-year-old broke clear of his rivals around the 36-kilometer mark and had a one-man journey until the finish line at Nagoya City Mizuho Park Athletic Stadium.

India's Sawan Barwal was 48 seconds behind in second, followed by Feng Peiyou of China who finished another 36 seconds behind. Yuya Yoshida of Japan was 11th.

"We've been talking about using this race as a stepping stone aiming for the Los Angeles Olympics, so it's really great to win the gold medal here," Yamashita said.

The Nagasaki Prefecture native was among the seven-man leading pack at around 30 km as he looked to break clear. But Han Il Ryong of North Korea, who eventually finished fourth, and Barwal quickly took over.

Yamashita patiently chased after the pair, however, and again moved in front at around 35 km before Han and then Barwal dropped off to pave the way for his victory.

"I simply couldn't go after them (initially). I thought I might be in a bit of trouble," Yamashita said. "But they weren't raising their pace as much as I expected and once I caught them, the only choice was to keep going as I'm not someone good toward the end."

Bahrain's Shitaye Habte won the women's title in 2:27:31, with China's Ciren Cuomu 12 seconds behind for silver.

Mikuni Yada, who represented Japan in the 10,000 meters at last year's world athletics championships in Tokyo, won bronze after finishing a further 13 seconds behind, with her compatriot Sayaka Sato coming fourth.

Eight runners were in the leading pack midway through the race before Yada and Indonesia's Rose Sitienei, who finished fifth, collided. Yada recovered to lead a four-runner pack with less than 10 km to go, but Habte took the lead in the final 5 km and ran away.

"I'm relieved to get the bronze medal, but it's a bit frustrating as I was aiming for the gold," said Yada, who set a marathon debutant national record of 2:19:57 in January at the Osaka Women's Marathon as she finished fourth.

The 26-year-old from Kumamoto Prefecture, where the magnitude-7.1 quake on July 28 killed more than 30 people, said she suffered right knee ligament damage a month ago and only had a week of training ahead of the Asian Games.

"Given I couldn't to train much, I was able to wrap things up well," she said. "I heard people close to me had to evacuate after the quake and the only thing I can do is give them courage through my running."
