FEATURE: Hyrox turns gym training into competition and community in Japan

By Takahito Tamura,KYODO NEWS
- Sep 26, 2026 - 09:44 All,Japan,Sports,Feature

CHIBA, Japan - For office worker Taiyo Kojima, working out at the gym had begun to feel aimless. Then he discovered Hyrox, a fitness race combining running and strength exercises, and suddenly he had something concrete to train for.

"After I started working, I lost my sense of purpose when training at the gym, but Hyrox gave me clear goals -- finishing the race and improving my time," said Kojima, 25, after competing in an event for the first time.

Immediately after crossing the finish line, however, his reaction was rather different.

"Honestly, it was brutal. I thought, 'I never want to do this again,'" said Kojima. But the feeling did not last.

"It's not often you have an opportunity to be in the spotlight like this, so the experience of being the center of attention felt amazing," he said. "Before I knew it, I was already thinking about working hard for the next race."

He was among about 12,000 people from more than 40 countries who took part in a four-day Hyrox event at Makuhari Messe in Chiba in early August.

With DJs pumping out upbeat music, competitors' names flashing across giant screens and spectators cheering from close to the course, the venue had an atmosphere blending sport and entertainment.

The event reflects a broader shift in Japan's growing fitness market, where exercise is increasingly becoming not just about building one's body but also about competing and connecting with others.

Founded in Germany in 2017, Hyrox requires competitors to alternate a 1-kilometer run with a strength-based exercise eight times, completing eight running segments and eight different workout stations. The exercises include pushing and pulling weighted sleds, rowing, carrying weights, lunges, burpee broad jumps and wall balls.

Organizers say 99 percent of entrants finish, with the average competitor taking about 90 minutes. There is no time limit, helping make the event accessible regardless of experience, and about 40 percent of those competing in Chiba were first-timers.

Kojima said training for Hyrox had also given him the sense that he had become what he called a "hybrid athlete," combining strength training with running.

Entrants can race individually, in two-person doubles or four-person relays, while there is also a division for athletes with disabilities. Celebrities and professional athletes have also been taking part.

Among the competitors in Chiba was Hitomi Kato, 70, from Shizuoka Prefecture, who won the women's 70-74 age category despite having little enthusiasm for sport when she was younger.

"I wasn't good at sports when I was young and I hated P.E.," she said with a smile. "What appeals to me is that you can take on the challenge at any age."

The Chiba competition was the third Hyrox event in Japan, following earlier races in Yokohama and Osaka, and the largest so far.

Spectators can watch from close to the action, some holding up message boards as they cheer competitors on.

Yoshikatsu Matsumura, a 52-year-old obstetrician-gynecologist from Gunma Prefecture who entered the men's individual race for the first time, described the experience as a "second youth."

"Training for it brings people at the gym together and creates a community outside of work and family life," he said. "There is also an element of satisfying people's desire for recognition, which I think makes it a sport well suited to the times."

Japan's fitness market was worth about 700 billion yen ($4.4 billion) in fiscal 2024, according to Teikoku Databank, with the number of fitness facilities also on the rise.

Steve Corbett, 41, a Hyrox competition operations manager, said cooperation with gyms around the country and increased exposure through social media had helped fuel its growth in Japan.

"It has become more than just a race," he said. "Its appeal is growing as a new kind of community culture that brings people together."

Hyrox's first event in Germany drew around 650 competitors, but this year's races across some 30 countries are expected to attract more than 1.7 million participants.

Organizers are also aiming for Hyrox to become an Olympic event sometime after the 2032 Brisbane Games.

"Next year, we want to hold an event in Japan with 20,000 participants," Corbett said.
