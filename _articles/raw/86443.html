Sumo: Onosato beaten again as Aonishiki wins to set up grand finale

KYODO NEWS
- Sep 26, 2026 - 19:46 All,Sports

TOKYO - Yokozuna Onosato was beaten for the second straight day Saturday while ozeki Aonishiki won to set up a fascinating finale to the Autumn Grand Sumo Tournament, with both wrestlers tied for the lead at 11-3.

Onosato, aware a win would preserve his one-win advantage, quickly went on the attack after securing a soft right underarm belt hold against ozeki Kotozakura (9-5) in the day's final bout at Tokyo's Ryogoku Kokugikan.

But Kotozakura, who remained calm under the early pressure, had more than enough time and space to thrust down the grand champion as he shifted his body to his left and sent the overnight leader crashing to the sand.

Onosato, troubled by left shoulder pain in recent meets, won the last of his five Emperor's Cups last September.

A bout earlier, Aonishiki made sure the title race would go down to the wire after finding his way past fellow ozeki Kirishima (7-7) for his eighth straight victory.

Ukrainian star Aonishiki, keeping his usual low-angled body position, seized a right overarm belt hold and then a left underarm grip to take control against Kirishima who desperately sought for an escape route.

The Mongolian-born grappler was tenacious and produced a beltless arm throw at the edge, but Aonishiki made sure his body could not be turned as both fell to the floor, the Ukrainian winning by a frontal crush out.

Aonishiki won July's Nagoya meet having been demoted to sekiwake, but returned to ozeki at the first time of asking after getting the required 10 wins in the process.

Former ozeki Asanoyama (10-4) fell out of contention by going down to new sekiwake Fujinokawa (10-4). Following a sharp opening clash, the No. 6 maegashira used his bigger frame and strong left arm to leave his opponent on the straw bales.

An agile Fujinokawa produced a last-ditch thrust down and while Asanoyama came up with a smart thigh grabbing push down on his way down, it was not enough to oust his opponent from the ring.
