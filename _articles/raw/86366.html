Japan, U.S. reliable allies with strong bond after WWII: Takaichi

KYODO NEWS
- Sep 25, 2026 - 19:32 All,Japan,World

TOKYO - Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Friday that Japan and the United States have become "allies bound by very strong ties" based on mutual trust after World War II, following a U.S.-China summit that noted the two nations' wartime cooperation.

Takaichi, meanwhile, told reporters that she has "no comment" on the meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Washington on Thursday.

"Although Japan and the United States once fought a war, we subsequently achieved genuine reconciliation and deepened our relationship," Takaichi said.

At Thursday's talks, Xi said that China and the United States "stood shoulder to shoulder in a fight against Japanese militarist aggressors," after Trump earlier that day called the two countries "allies in World War II," which ended in 1945 with Japan's defeat.

Ties between Japan and China have been strained since Takaichi, who took office last October, said the following month that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could prompt a response by the Self-Defense Forces, infuriating Beijing and leading to its political and economic reprisals against Tokyo.

Takaichi also said Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio will remain in communication over the International Criminal Court issue. Its Japanese head, Tomoko Akane, has faced sanctions by Washington, including asset freezes and travel bans.

The Trump administration has accused the ICC of encroaching on state sovereignty and has aggressively pursued a campaign to dismantle the Hague-based body, opposing its probes into alleged war crimes committed by U.S. military personnel and Israel.

"We recognize that the United States has a very strong sense of distrust toward the ICC and its stance is extremely tough," Takaichi said, while stressing that she told Trump Japan has "consistently supported" the court and she had "frank exchanges of views" with him at Tuesday's summit.
